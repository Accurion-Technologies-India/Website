Concrete Quality Grading via Pulse Velocity

Criterion for concrete quality grading based on ultrasonic pulse velocity per IS 13311 (Part 1): 1992 (Reaffirmed 2018) Table 2 for structural concrete members.

Sl. No. Pulse Velocity (km/second) Concrete Quality Grading Typical Structural Condition
1 Above 4.5 km/s (> 4500 m/s) Excellent High-density dense matrix, virtually free of voids, cracks, or honeycombing.
2 3.5 to 4.5 km/s (3500 – 4500 m/s) Good Standard structural concrete meeting design specifications with sound consolidation.
3 3.0 to 3.5 km/s (3000 – 3500 m/s) Medium Moderate quality, potential slight porosity, low compaction, or micro-cracking.
4 Below 3.0 km/s (< 3000 m/s) Doubtful Significant internal honeycombs, large voids, delamination, or severe damage.

UPV Transducer Arrangement & Transmission Arrangements

Ultrasonic pulse velocity is calculated as V = L / T, where L is the path length between transducers and T is the transit time. Depending on site access, three transducer arrangements are utilized:

1. Direct Transmission

Transducers on opposite parallel surfaces. Maximum pulse energy transmission; highest diagnostic accuracy for columns, beams, and slabs.

2. Semi-Direct Transmission

Transducers placed on adjacent perpendicular surfaces (e.g. beam corners). Excellent when only corner access is accessible.

3. Indirect / Surface Transmission

Both transducers on the same surface. Used for pavement slabs, retaining walls, and determining depth of surface cracks.
