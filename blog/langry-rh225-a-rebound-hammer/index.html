Langry RH225-A Rebound Hammer: Complete Guide, Specifications & Applications

NDT Testing Type N • 2.207 J Published: October 9, 2026 • 6 min read • By Accurion Technical Team

Introduction

The LANGRY RH225-A is a high-precision mechanical concrete test hammer designed specifically for non-destructive evaluation (NDT) of hardened concrete in structural engineering, infrastructure development, and site quality control.

For civil engineers, site managers, testing laboratories, and structural auditors looking for a dependable, cost-friendly, and field-ready concrete test hammer, understanding the technical specifications, operating principles, calibration tolerances, and intended applications of the RH225-A is essential before making an equipment investment.

What is the LANGRY RH225-A?

The RH225-A is a standard Type N mechanical rebound hammer manufactured by LANGRY. The instrument functions on the spring-driven impact and rebound principle (originally pioneered by Ernst Schmidt): a spring-loaded mass with a defined energy impacts against the concrete surface through a plunge rod, and the rebound distance of the mass is recorded along a graduated linear scale by an indicator pointer.

The magnitude of the rebound value (the "Rebound Index" or R-value) correlates directly with the surface hardness of the concrete, which in turn provides an empirical estimation of its in-situ compressive strength and structural uniformity.

Standards & Testing Codes Associated with RH225-A

The RH225-A conforms to all prominent Indian and international engineering standards governing non-destructive rebound hammer testing:

  • IS 13311 (Part 2): 1992 (India): Non-destructive testing of concrete — Methods of test: Part 2 Rebound Hammer. Sets out requirements for testing procedure, surface preparation, angle correction, and estimation of compressive strength.
  • ASTM C805 / C805M (USA): Standard Test Method for Rebound Number of Hardened Concrete.
  • EN 12504-2 & EN 13791 (Europe): Testing concrete in structures — Part 2: Determination of rebound number & Assessment of in-situ compressive strength.
  • JIS A 1155 (Japan): Method of test for rebound number of concrete.
  • ISO/DIS 8045: International standard for rebound number determinations.
  • JGJ/T 23: Technical specification for inspecting concrete compressive strength by the rebound method.

Step-by-Step Practical Testing Walkthrough

Obtaining reliable, reproducible test results with the RH225-A requires strict adherence to standardized testing methodology per IS 13311 (Part 2):

  1. Surface Preparation: Test surfaces must be clean, smooth, dry, and free from loose mortar, plaster, slurry, or honeycombing. Grind the testing patch smooth using the carborundum abrasive stone supplied in the RH225-A kit.
  2. Establish a Testing Grid: Mark out a test area of approximately 300 mm × 300 mm. Mark at least 10 to 12 individual test points spaced at least 25 to 30 mm apart and away from free edges or heavily clustered rebars.
  3. Position and Impact: Hold the hammer perpendicular to the concrete surface (θ = 0° for vertical walls, −90° downwards for slabs, or +90° upwards for soffits). Depress the plunge rod firmly against the surface until the internal hammer releases and strikes.
  4. Record and Apply Corrections: Press the pushbutton on the side to lock the pointer and read the rebound value on the graduated scale. Apply angle correction factors if testing at an inclined or overhead angle.
  5. Outlier Rejection & Averaging: Per IS 13311 (Part 2), discard any readings that deviate by more than ±6 units from the preliminary average, and recalculate the mean of the remaining readings.

Concrete Quality Grading as per IS 13311 (Part 2)

  • Average Rebound Number > 40: Very Good to Excellent Hard Concrete (High compressive strength)
  • Average Rebound Number 30 – 40: Good Layer Quality (Standard structural grade)
  • Average Rebound Number 20 – 30: Fair / Questionable Quality (Further core test or UPV test advised)
  • Average Rebound Number < 20: Poor / Delaminated / Suspect Concrete

Why Calibration Matters: The Certified Test Anvil

Like any precision mechanical measuring instrument, rebound hammers must be calibrated and verified on a standardized steel test anvil before and after each testing series.

LANGRY specifies that on an authorized calibration steel anvil (hardness HRC 58 to 62, mass ≥ 16 kg), the RH225-A must yield a calibration index of:

Certified Anvil Calibration Value: 80 ± 2

If the hammer yields readings outside the 78–82 range on the certified test anvil, internal cleaning, spring adjustment, or servicing is necessary before undertaking field tests.
